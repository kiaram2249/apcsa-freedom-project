### Content

This year in APCSA, I had the opportunity to develop both my programming skills and my problem-solving abilities through the Freedom Project. Throughout the year, I learned Java and strengthened my understanding of important programming concepts such as classes, objects, methods, loops, arrays, and inheritance. These skills gave me a strong foundation that helped me approach school projects with more confidence. One of the most challenging but rewarding experiences was learning how to use Godot, a game engine that I explored with my partner Robert for my Freedom Project. Since Godot was completely new to me, I spent a lot of time reading documentation, watching tutorials, and experimenting with different features to understand how everything worked.

Learning Godot taught me much more than just game development. It improved my ability to research solutions, debug errors, and think critically when problems arose. There were many moments when features did not work as expected, but instead of giving up, I learned how to break problems into smaller pieces and test different solutions until I found one that worked. As I became more familiar with Godot, I learned how to create scenes, scripts, user interfaces, and game mechanics that helped bring my project to life.

The Freedom Project allowed me to combine everything I learned throughout the year into one final product. Building a game from scratch with Robert required creativity, planning, and persistence. It also taught me and Robert valuable time-management skills because we had to balance learning a new tool while continuing to develop and improve our project. However, throughout the process I documented my progress, and reflected on challenges.
